About Me

I study data science at Northeastern University, and the rest of my time goes to being somewhere else with a camera. Travel is not a break from how I think. It is the same habit: show up, look longer than is comfortable, and decide what the frame should hold.

Photography is the record. I have shot across ten countries, and the pictures I keep are usually ordinary. A street before it fills. A table. A window on a train. I care more about composition than about a landmark. A photograph works when you can tell what was left out.

Two of those trips were Dialogue of Civilizations programs. In the summer of 2023 I studied in Croatia, in a course on cross-cultural psychology built around the breakup of Yugoslavia. The program also moved through Bosnia. We visited NGOs and historical centers and talked with people who work on memory after a war. I photographed more than I took notes. In the summer of 2024 I was in Belgium, for longer, studying how institutions use data. We heard from people at NATO, Eurostat, and the European Parliament about how a number becomes a policy. Brussels is not a postcard city. I liked it for that.

Shorter trips sit around those two. A week in Spain, Denmark, the Netherlands, and Italy, each. A week is enough to get lost and not enough to feel local. The travel page turns that into a count of days, which is a blunter record than the photographs and still the one I can draw. If you want to reach me, the links below are the direct ones.
